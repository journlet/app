# Intent Statement: Quick Task Entry

## Problem Statement

Journlet is an installed web app, so a person has to open it before anything else; from the moment it is open, adding a new task should be as quick as possible. [desc]

Adding a task today takes too long in three places: getting to the point of typing (the entry bar, then a full-screen form), a form that offers more choices than a quick task needs, and having to be on, or pick, the right day, week, month or year first. [Q1]

## Target Customer

The people who use Journlet, including the owner, benefit by getting a task down before the thought is lost. [Q2] [Q5]

## Success Metrics

- With the app already open, a task is saved with one tap, then typing, then Enter. [Q3]
- A short task is saved within about 5 seconds of the app being open. [Q3]
- Both measures must hold; meeting only one is not enough. [Q3]
- They matter most on the day page and the week page. [Q4]

## Initiative Trigger

Tasks slip because adding one feels like too much effort in the moment. [Q5]

## Initial Scope Signal

- Workflow-selected scope: `quick-capture`. [scope]
- User-confirmed boundary: explore a few quick-entry options, choose one, then specify, build and test it. [Q8]
- The owner's starting idea is to add a task straight onto a section (day, week, month or year) with the keyboard opening in place, then either press Enter to save or use a button to edit it further; the owner is open to other suggestions. [desc]
